\section{FRFP Case Study (Science / Discovery): AlphaFold}
\label{app:frfp-alphafold}

This appendix analyses DeepMind’s AlphaFold as an FRFP case study in
long-horizon scientific discovery.
We focus on how AlphaFold’s design and deployment interact with the FRFP
architecture: explicit/tacit separation, boundary design, safe horizons, and
institutional semantics in the life sciences.

\subsection{Background}

Predicting a protein’s three-dimensional structure from its amino-acid
sequence was a central open problem in structural biology for roughly
50~years.
Experimental techniques such as X-ray crystallography, NMR, and cryo-EM are
precise but slow, expensive, and unevenly distributed across labs.

In 2020, at the CASP14 (Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction)
competition, DeepMind’s AlphaFold2 demonstrated unprecedented accuracy:
for many targets it predicted backbone atom positions with median error
$\lesssim 1$~\AA, substantially outperforming all other methods and reaching
a level CASP’s organisers described as effectively ``solving'' the protein
folding problem for many single-chain proteins.

Subsequently:

\begin{itemize}
  \item DeepMind and EMBL–EBI released the AlphaFold Protein Structure
        Database (AF-DB), which by 2024 covered over 214~million protein
        sequences across most known organisms, a $\sim$500-fold expansion
        relative to the initial 2021 release.
  \item AF-DB increased structural coverage of the human proteome from
        roughly 48\% to about 76\% of residues, dramatically shrinking the
        set of proteins with no structural information.
  \item AlphaFold and related tools have been used in thousands of research
        projects, including studies of nuclear pore complexes, malaria and
        neglected disease targets, and antibiotic resistance, and are now
        integrated into mainstream structural biology workflows.
  \item AlphaFold~3, released in 2024, extends predictions to protein
        complexes and interactions with ligands, nucleic acids, and
        antibodies, targeting drug design more directly.
\end{itemize}

This makes AlphaFold an outstanding example of AI success that is tightly
coupled to human scientific practice and data ecosystems, rather than a
standalone agent.

\subsection{Explicit and Tacit Spaces in Structural Biology}

\subsubsection*{Explicit space \(E_{\mathrm{AF}}\)}

For AlphaFold and AF-DB, explicit space comprises:

\begin{itemize}
  \item amino-acid sequences from UniProt / UniRef and other databases;
  \item multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) and evolutionary couplings;
  \item experimentally determined structures in the Protein Data Bank (PDB);
  \item AlphaFold’s network parameters and prediction code;
  \item predicted 3D structures, per-residue confidence scores (pLDDT),
        and predicted aligned error (PAE) matrices;
  \item AF-DB, which serves predictions and metadata via web, APIs, and bulk
        download.
\end{itemize}

These are all artefacts in \(E_{\mathrm{AF}}\):
bits and tensors that can be processed by machines, versioned, and shared.

\subsubsection*{Tacit space \(T_{\mathrm{bio}}\)}

Tacit space is the community’s scientific understanding:

\begin{itemize}
  \item structural biologists’ intuition about protein domains, flexibility,
        disorder, and function;
  \item medicinal chemists’ sense of ligandability, binding-site quality,
        and risk of false positives in virtual screening;
  \item experimentalists’ judgement about which predictions are good enough
        to guide experiments, and where empirical validation is essential;
  \item institutions’ and funders’ beliefs about the value and limitations
        of in~silico structure prediction in drug discovery pipelines.
\end{itemize}

Correctness in science is ultimately judged in this tacit space:
by whether findings replicate, support plausible mechanistic narratives, and
fit into broader theoretical and empirical structures.

\subsection{Boundary Design: Predictions vs.\ Scientific Claims}

\subsubsection*{Formal interface: predictions with uncertainty}

AlphaFold’s design and release strategy incorporate explicit boundary
elements between its outputs and scientific conclusions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Each structure prediction comes with per-residue confidence scores
        (pLDDT) and PAE maps, making uncertainty explicit and machine- and
        human-readable.
  \item AF-DB documentation clearly states that predictions are not a
        substitute for experiments, especially for low-confidence regions,
        disordered proteins, and conformationally dynamic systems.
  \item The system is framed as providing structural hypotheses and
        starting points for further work, not definitive answers about
        function or druggability.
\end{itemize}

In FRFP terms, the boundary functor \(J{-}K\) maps explicit predictions in
\(E_{\mathrm{AF}}\) into tacit states \(T_{\mathrm{bio}}\) via:

\begin{itemize}
  \item scientists’ interpretation of pLDDT/PAE as reliability signals;
  \item domain-specific heuristics about when to trust or discount
        AlphaFold models;
  \item peer-review and replication processes that convert structural
        hypotheses into accepted knowledge.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{De facto use: high trust, but still mediated}

Empirically, the community has come to treat AlphaFold structures as:

\begin{itemize}
  \item near-experimental quality for many well-behaved, single-chain,
        globular proteins with high-confidence scores;
  \item less reliable for intrinsically disordered regions, flexible loops,
        large complexes, and ligand-bound conformations;
  \item excellent starting models for phasing crystallographic data or
        building cryo-EM models, substantially accelerating standard
        experimental pipelines.
\end{itemize}

From an FRFP standpoint:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the boundary remains with human scientists and experimentalists;
  \item AlphaFold outputs strongly inform tacit reasoning but are not, in
        general, treated as final claims about mechanism or drug efficacy;
  \item this arrangement---highly capable explicit system plus robust tacit
        interpretation---is relatively close to an FRFP-aligned design.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Protocol-Level Properties in FRFP Terms}

\subsubsection*{Z.9.1 Explicit-Space Strength:
  A Mature, Well-Specified Semantics}

AlphaFold’s explicit semantics are unusually strong for an AI system:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The task is well-defined:
        map a sequence to a static 3D structure under standard physical
        conventions, up to known caveats (flexibility, alternate states).
  \item Ground truth exists:
        experimentally-determined structures provide a rich, high-quality,
        globally shared reference database (PDB).
  \item Evaluation protocols (CASP) offer strict, blind metrics for accuracy,
        with community acceptance of what counts as ``good enough''.
\end{itemize}

From an FRFP perspective:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the mapping
        \(
          f_{\mathrm{AF}} : \text{sequence, MSAs} \to \text{structure}
        \)
        has a clear semantics and strong external validation;
  \item $E_{\mathrm{AF}}$ is unusually close to a self-contained domain of
        explicit artefacts whose correctness can be assessed without
        extensive recourse to tacit local context;
  \item this is precisely the kind of problem where FRFP expects explicit AI
        to be able to ``go far'' safely before requiring tacit correction.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Z.9.2 Explicit Transparency: Confidence and Coverage}

Key design choices that support FRFP-style use:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Per-residue pLDDT scores and PAE maps provide structured, explicit
        uncertainty, allowing downstream users to modulate trust.
  \item AF-DB exposes coverage and confidence statistics across proteomes,
        clarifying where the system is strong vs.\ weak.
  \item Documentation emphasises limitations: dynamics, complexes, and ligand
        binding are only partially captured, especially for AlphaFold2-era
        models.
\end{itemize}

This explicit communication of uncertainty and domain of competence is an
instance of FRFP’s recommendation that explicit pipelines carry their own
self-descriptions and limits, making boundary decisions in \(T_{\mathrm{bio}}\)
better informed.

\subsubsection*{Z.9.3 Safe Horizons in Scientific Use}

Although AlphaFold is powerful, its safe usage horizons are still bounded:

\begin{itemize}
  \item For many basic structural biology tasks (e.g.\ annotating domain
        architectures, suggesting mutational impacts on stability),
        predictions can be used relatively directly, within the guardrails of
        pLDDT/PAE.
  \item For mechanistic inferences and drug discovery (binding energetics,
        allosteric regulation, conformational ensembles), AlphaFold outputs
        are starting points that must be combined with other modelling and
        experimental data.
  \item Structural biology remains an iterative loop:
        predictions suggest experiments; experiments confirm, refine, or
        refute predictions; tacit understanding evolves accordingly.
\end{itemize}

FRFP’s safe-horizon lens thus suggests:

\begin{itemize}
  \item AlphaFold enables very long runs in explicit structure space, but
        tacit scientific correctness is safeguarded by experimental
        validation and peer review;
  \item attempts to push AlphaFold beyond these horizons---e.g.\ to fully
        automate target validation and drug design without adequate data---are
        precisely where current AI drug discovery efforts have struggled.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Institutional and Ecosystem-Level Semantics}

\subsubsection*{Open Data and Shared Infrastructure}

AlphaFold’s impact is amplified by institutional choices:

\begin{itemize}
  \item AF-DB is freely accessible and integrated with UniProt, InterPro,
        and other resources, functioning as a public infrastructure layer for
        structural biology.
  \item This enables a large population of scientists to build tacit
        understanding of when and how to use predictions, via thousands of
        papers and lab practices.
  \item OpenFold and related community projects extend and reimplement
        AlphaFold-like models, allowing independent scrutiny and adaptation.
\end{itemize}

In FRFP terms, these institutions act as operators on the global tacit state
of the structural biology community, spreading knowledge of AlphaFold’s
capabilities and limits and helping to stabilise the explicit/tacit interface.

\subsubsection*{Drug Discovery and Commercial Ecosystem}

AlphaFold has also shaped commercial AI-for-drug-discovery:

\begin{itemize}
  \item companies like Isomorphic Labs and startups such as Latent Labs build
        on AlphaFold-style models to design drugs and synthetic proteins;
  \item large pharma consortia are now pooling structure data (e.g.\
        OpenFold3 collaborations) to train next-generation structural models,
        explicitly citing AlphaFold as a foundation.
  \item industry analysts highlight AlphaFold as a key bright spot in an
        otherwise slower-than-hyped AI drug discovery landscape.
\end{itemize}

Here, FRFP’s institutional semantics emphasise:

\begin{itemize}
  \item firms are treating AlphaFold outputs as high-quality explicit
        artefacts that feed into broader, still-challenging tacit tasks
        (biology, chemistry, clinical development);
  \item where companies attempt to over-automate tacit scientific judgment
        (e.g.\ fully AI-driven target selection with limited wet-lab work),
        failures and delays mirror the non-automation claims in the main text.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Counterfactual: FRFP Pitfalls AlphaFold Mostly Avoided}

It is instructive to note which FRFP failure modes AlphaFold largely
\emph{avoided}, compared to some failures in other case studies:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Misplaced boundaries.}
        AlphaFold was framed, and is mostly used, as a prediction tool feeding
        into human-led science, not as an autonomous decision-maker about
        patient care or investment. The boundary stays with scientists.
  \item \textbf{Opaque or uncalibrated output.}
        Confidence measures and evaluation benchmarks are built into the
        explicit interface, reducing the risk of uncalibrated over-trust.
  \item \textbf{Weak explicit semantics.}
        The problem definition (static structure prediction) and evaluation
        are unusually clean, unlike in domains such as legal reasoning or
        clinical decision-making, where semantics are more entangled with tacit
        norms.
  \item \textbf{Closed, proprietary governance.}
        By partnering with EMBL–EBI and releasing AF-DB openly, DeepMind
        effectively created a shared institutional operator that diffuses
        control and supports community governance and critique.
\end{itemize}

These design and deployment choices align AlphaFold more closely with FRFP’s
principles than many other high-profile AI systems.

\subsection{Lessons for FRFP and Scientific AI}

AlphaFold provides a constructive counterpart to failure case studies:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Explicit problems with strong ground truth are fertile ground.}
        FRFP predicts that domains with clear explicit semantics and robust
        evaluation (like structure prediction) are particularly suitable for
        powerful explicit AI systems.

  \item \textbf{Uncertainty-aware interfaces support healthy boundaries.}
        By surfacing confidence and error explicitly, AlphaFold helps
        scientists keep correctness in tacit space, rather than blindly
        trusting predictions.

  \item \textbf{Open infrastructure shapes population-level tacit states.}
        AF-DB and community reimplementations act as institutional operators
        that propagate good usage norms and collective understanding of
        limitations.

  \item \textbf{Non-automation still applies downstream.}
        Even with AlphaFold’s success, drug discovery and mechanistic biology
        remain only partially automatable; tacit scientific judgment and
        experimentation remain structurally indispensable.

  \item \textbf{Formal FRFP analysis helps generalise from success.}
        By analysing why AlphaFold works as a socio-technical system---not just
        as a model---FRFP provides guidance for designing future scientific AI
        tools that integrate explicit strength with tacit, institutional
        oversight.
\end{enumerate}

In this sense, AlphaFold is both a triumph of explicit AI in a well-chosen
domain and a demonstration of how FRFP-compatible infrastructure can amplify
an AI breakthrough into a durable, community-wide scientific advance.
