## 3.1 Overview

This chapter defines the syntactic layer of FRFP — the Task Decomposition Grammar (TDG) that generates pipeline terms — and the semantic reduction rules that normalize them. We prove two structural properties: termination (every reduction sequence is finite) and confluence (the order of reductions does not affect the final result). Together these establish that every pipeline term has a unique normal form modulo navigation equivalence.

---

## 3.2 Task Decomposition Grammar

**Definition 3.1** (TDG terms). A TDG term over the kernel primitives is a well-typed finite sequence:

$$t ::= \varepsilon \mid p \cdot t$$

where $p$ is a primitive and $t$ is a TDG term, subject to the typing constraint that consecutive primitives compose (the target of $p$ equals the source of the first primitive of $t$).

TDG terms are syntactic objects. A TDG term *denotes* a pipeline (morphism) in $\mathbf{C}_0$ when interpreted by the semantic function $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket$ (Chapter 4).

**Definition 3.2** (Free category). The free category $\mathbf{F}$ generated by the six primitives has:
- Objects: $\{\varnothing, E_0, T_0\}$
- Morphisms: all well-typed finite compositions of primitives

$\mathbf{F}$ is the syntactic model; $\mathbf{C}_0$ is its semantic quotient.

## 3.3 Reduction Relations

**Definition 3.3** (Explicit reduction). A reduction relation $\to_e$ on TDG terms captures explicit-space simplifications: rewriting consecutive AI-executable steps into a canonical form. Concretely:

$$\mathrm{EC} \cdot \mathrm{EC} \to_e \mathrm{EC},\qquad \mathrm{ED} \cdot \mathrm{EC} \to_e \mathrm{ED}$$

together with similar rules for all combinations of $\{\mathrm{RI}, \mathrm{EC}, \mathrm{ED}\}$.

**Definition 3.4** (Tacit reduction). A reduction relation $\to_t$ on TDG terms captures tacit-space simplifications:

$$\mathrm{TE} \cdot \mathrm{TE} \to_t \mathrm{TE},\qquad \mathrm{HFD} \cdot \mathrm{TE} \to_t \mathrm{HFD}$$

**Definition 3.5** (Combined reduction). The combined reduction $\to_c = \to_e \cup \to_t$, with contextual closure (reduction may happen inside any context). The reflexive-transitive closure is written $\twoheadrightarrow_c$.

## 3.4 Normal Forms

**Definition 3.6** (Normal form). A TDG term $t$ is in *normal form* if no reduction rule applies to it. A normal form has the structure:

$$t_{\mathrm{nf}} = \underbrace{e_1 \cdots e_k}_{\text{explicit block}} \cdot \underbrace{\mathrm{RB}^?}_{\text{optional boundary}} \cdot \underbrace{h_1 \cdots h_m}_{\text{tacit block}}$$

where each $e_i \in \{\mathrm{RI}, \mathrm{EC}, \mathrm{ED}\}$, $\mathrm{RB}^?$ is either $\mathrm{RB}$ or absent, and each $h_j \in \{\mathrm{TE}, \mathrm{HFD}\}$.

**[Lean: `Frfp.Minimal.BasisSweep.PrimitiveTraceAdmissibleStrong`]**

This three-block structure is the **protocol normal form**. It reflects the operational semantics of an FRFP interaction: explicit work first, then (optionally) handoff to human tacit evaluation.

## 3.5 Termination

**Theorem 3.1** (Termination). Every reduction sequence in $\to_c$ is finite.

*Proof sketch*. Define a measure $\mu(t)$ as the number of consecutive same-phase pairs in $t$ (pairs of adjacent primitives in the same subcategory $\mathbf{E}_{cat}$ or $\mathbf{T}_{cat}$ that can be merged). Each reduction step strictly decreases $\mu(t)$. Since $\mu(t) \geq 0$, all sequences terminate. □

## 3.6 Local Confluence

**Definition 3.7** (Local confluence). The relation $\to_c$ is *locally confluent* if for any $t, t_1, t_2$ with $t \to_c t_1$ and $t \to_c t_2$, there exists $t_3$ with $t_1 \twoheadrightarrow_c t_3$ and $t_2 \twoheadrightarrow_c t_3$.

**Theorem 3.2** (Local confluence of $\to_c$). The combined reduction is locally confluent.

*Proof*. By case analysis on the pairs of one-step reductions from $t$. Since explicit and tacit reductions act on disjoint parts of the term (by the ETS theorem, there are no cross-phase interactions), any two one-step reductions are either:
- In the same phase at non-overlapping positions (trivially confluent),
- In the same phase at overlapping positions (resolved by the phase-specific confluence lemma),
- In different phases (independently applicable in either order).

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ConfluenceProof.diamond_property`]**

**Key lemma used**: Lifted action commutativity — navigation actions commute with explicit reduction. This is axiomatized from the operational semantics. **[Lean: `Frfp.Core.Confluence.lifted_action_commutes`]**

> *Note*: Lifted action commutativity is implicit in the original published theory. The Lean formalization makes it an explicit required lemma for the confluence proof.

## 3.7 Global Confluence (Church–Rosser)

**Theorem 3.3** (Confluence). By Newman's Lemma, termination (Theorem 3.1) together with local confluence (Theorem 3.2) implies global confluence:

$$\forall t, t_1, t_2,\quad t \twoheadrightarrow_c t_1 \wedge t \twoheadrightarrow_c t_2 \Rightarrow \exists t_3,\quad t_1 \twoheadrightarrow_c t_3 \wedge t_2 \twoheadrightarrow_c t_3$$

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ConfluenceProof.confluence`]**  
*Source*: Newman's Lemma, [Newman 1942]; abstract reduction systems, [Baader–Nipkow 1998] Ch. 2.

## 3.8 Normal Form Uniqueness (Corrected)

**Theorem 3.4** (Normal form uniqueness modulo navigation equivalence). For any TDG term $t$, all normal forms reachable from $t$ are navigation-equivalent:

$$\forall t,\ \forall \mathrm{nf}_1, \mathrm{nf}_2,\quad t \twoheadrightarrow_c \mathrm{nf}_1 \wedge t \twoheadrightarrow_c \mathrm{nf}_2 \wedge \mathrm{IsNF}(\mathrm{nf}_1) \wedge \mathrm{IsNF}(\mathrm{nf}_2) \Rightarrow \mathrm{nf}_1 \equiv_\nabla \mathrm{nf}_2$$

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.SemanticCorrectness.unique_nf_mod_nav`]**

> **Lean correction**: The original published theory states normal forms are *literally unique* (equal). The Lean formalization shows they are unique only *modulo navigation equivalence* $\equiv_\nabla$. Two normal forms from the same term may differ in navigation annotations while denoting the same pipeline. The corrected statement is given above.

**Definition 3.8** (Navigation equivalence). Two terms $t_1 \equiv_\nabla t_2$ if they differ only in navigation morphisms (admissible path annotations added by the navigation layer). Navigation morphisms do not change the computational content of a term. **[Lean: `Frfp.Core.SemanticCorrectness.NavEquivalent`]**

## 3.9 Semantic Correctness

**Theorem 3.5** (Observable invariance). For any term $t$ and its normal form $\mathrm{nf}$, the observable correctness judgment is preserved:

$$\mathrm{obs}(t) = \mathrm{obs}(\mathrm{nf})$$

where $\mathrm{obs} = \gamma \circ \mathrm{Sem} \circ \mathrm{initial}$ is the composition of semantic evaluation, correctness judgment extraction, and trajectory initialization. **[Lean: `Frfp.Core.SemanticCorrectness.obsCorrect_invariant`]**

**Definition 3.9** (Correctness judgment type). The correctness judgment type has three values:

$$\mathrm{CorrectnessJudgment} = \{\mathrm{correct},\ \mathrm{incorrect},\ \mathrm{unknown}\}$$

This is a new type made explicit by the Lean formalization; it was implicit in the original paper. **[Lean: `Frfp.Core.TacitDependence.CorrectnessJudgment`]**

## 3.10 Protocol Normal Form Theorem

The protocol normal form theorem (a novel result from the formalization) strengthens the three-block structure result:

**Theorem 3.6** (Protocol normal form). Any strongly admissible primitive trace decomposes as an explicit block, optional boundary crossing, and tacit block:

$$\forall \mathcal{T},\ \mathrm{AdmissibleStrong}(\mathcal{T}) \Rightarrow \exists e_1 \ldots e_k,\ h_1 \ldots h_m,\ b \in \{\top, \bot\},\ \mathcal{T} = [e_1, \ldots, e_k] \mathbin{+\!\!+} (\text{if } b \text{ then } [\mathrm{RB}] \text{ else } []) \mathbin{+\!\!+} [h_1, \ldots, h_m]$$

**[Lean: `Frfp.Minimal.BasisSweep.protocol_normal_form_theorem`]** — Machine-checked proof.

This theorem is stated precisely as the Lean source defines it, making the three-block decomposition a verified property of all admissible traces.
