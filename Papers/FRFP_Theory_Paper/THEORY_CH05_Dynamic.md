## 5.1 Overview

This chapter covers two connected aspects of FRFP dynamics:

1. **Epistemic state and credence degradation** — how tacit knowledge degrades over time when not refreshed by human judgment.
2. **Probabilistic stopping times and survival analysis** — the stochastic model of pipeline resolution, connecting to the `runToω` bijection.

Both areas contain Lean-specific precision improvements: credence bounds and degradation clamping (corrections from formalization) and five fully-proven probability theorems (results that were axiomatized in an earlier version and later proved via Mathlib).

## 5.2 Epistemic State

**Definition 5.1** (Tacit state). A tacit state records the current quality of human-grounded knowledge in a pipeline execution. It is represented as a record with fields including a credence value and a degradation parameter.

**Definition 5.2** (Credence). The credence of a tacit state is a rational number:

$$c \in [0, 1] \subset \mathbb{Q}$$

> **Lean correction (P-07)**: The original paper uses credence notation without stating bounds explicitly. The Lean formalization enforces bounds by construction:

```lean
structure Credence where
  value : Rat
  nonneg : 0 ≤ value
  le_one : value ≤ 1
```

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.InstitutionalLayer.Credence`]**

Without these structural bounds, any arithmetic on credence could produce values outside $[0,1]$, making the tacit degradation theorem unprovable.

## 5.3 Degradation

**Definition 5.3** (Degradation operator $\delta$). The degradation operator models the decay of tacit correctness over time when not refreshed by human evaluation:

$$\delta : \mathrm{TacitState} \to \mathrm{TacitState}$$

**Definition 5.4** (Degradation formula — corrected). Given credence $c \in [0,1]$ and degradation rate $d \geq 0$, after $t$ steps the credence is:

$$c'(t) = \max\!\Big(0,\ \min\!\Big(1,\ c - d \cdot t\Big)\Big)$$

> **Lean correction (P-07)**: The original paper writes $c'(t) = c - d \cdot t$ without clamping. The Lean formalization requires explicit clamping to keep credence within $[0,1]$; without it, the non-negativity proof obligation fails.

**Theorem 5.1** (Degradation monotonicity). Credence is monotonically non-increasing under repeated degradation:
$$t_1 \leq t_2 \Rightarrow c'(t_2) \leq c'(t_1)$$

*Proof*. Follows from the clamped formula: the $\max(0, \cdot)$ preserves monotonicity of the underlying linear decrease. □

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.DynamicLayer.degradation_monotone`]**

## 5.4 Entropy Drift

**Definition 5.5** (Entropy drift). The entropy of the tacit component of a pipeline increases monotonically when no human evaluation step occurs between AI steps:

$$\Delta H = H(t_2) - H(t_1) \geq 0 \quad \text{for } t_1 \leq t_2 \text{ with no HFD/TE between them}$$

**Theorem 5.2** (Entropy drift monotonicity). Under continuous AI-only execution (no $\mathrm{TE}$ or $\mathrm{HFD}$ steps), the entropy of the tacit component is non-decreasing.

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.DynamicLayer.entropy_drift_monotone`]**

Entropy drift formalizes the FRFP claim that purely AI-driven pipelines without human checkpoints progressively lose tacit validity. The $\mathrm{TE}$ primitive resets the drift by incorporating human evaluation.

## 5.5 Stopping Times

**Definition 5.6** (Stopping time). A stopping time $\tau$ for pipeline $P$ is a measurable random variable:
$$\tau : \Omega \to \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$$

such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the event $\{\tau \leq n\}$ is measurable with respect to the filtration generated by the first $n$ steps of the run.

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.Probability.StoppingTime`]** (axiomatized; measurability requires the `runToω` measure-space connection).

In the FRFP context, $\tau$ is the step at which the pipeline reaches a normal form — the first step where no further reduction is possible.

**Definition 5.7** (Hazard rate). The hazard rate $h(n)$ is the conditional probability that the pipeline resolves at step $n$, given it has not resolved before step $n$:
$$h(n) = \mathbb{P}(\tau = n \mid \tau \geq n) = \frac{S(n-1) - S(n)}{S(n-1)}$$

where $S$ is the survival function.

**Definition 5.8** (Survival function). The survival function $S(n)$ is the probability that the pipeline has not yet resolved by step $n$:
$$S(n) = \mathbb{P}(\tau > n)$$

## 5.6 Five Proven Probability Theorems

The following five theorems were axiomatized in an earlier version of the formalization (March 2026) and subsequently proved using Lean's Mathlib library (April 2026). They are now machine-checked theorems, not assumptions.

**Theorem 5.3** (Survival product formula).
$$S(N) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (1 - h(k))$$

*Proof*. By induction on $N$, using the definition of conditional probability and the product rule for independent events. □  
**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ProbabilityProven.survival_product_formula`]**

**Theorem 5.4** (Hazard–survival relation).
$$h(n) = \frac{S(n-1) - S(n)}{S(n-1)}$$

*Proof*. Unfolding the definition of conditional probability and cancelling. □  
**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ProbabilityProven.hazard_survival_relation`]**

**Theorem 5.5** (Survival monotonicity).
$$N \leq M \Rightarrow S(M) \leq S(N)$$

*Proof*. The survival function is non-increasing: conditioning on later resolution can only decrease the probability of not yet resolving. □  
**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ProbabilityProven.survival_monotone`]**

**Theorem 5.6** (Geometric survival). When the hazard rate is constant, $h(n) = h$ for all $n$:
$$S(n) = (1-h)^{n+1}$$

*Proof*. By induction, applying the product formula with constant hazard. □  
**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ProbabilityProven.geometric_survival`]**

**Theorem 5.7** (Infinite product limit). When $h(k) > 0$ for all $k$:
$$\prod_{k=0}^{\infty} (1 - h(k)) = 0$$

*Proof*. For any $h(k) \geq \epsilon > 0$, the product $(1-\epsilon)^N \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$. □  
**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.ProbabilityProven.infinite_product_limit`]**

> *Significance of these proofs*: Their transition from axioms to theorems demonstrates that the FRFP probabilistic foundations are sound — the original axioms were conservative placeholders, not foundational compromises. As Lean's Mathlib matures, further axiom reduction is possible.

## 5.7 Safe Horizon

**Definition 5.9** (Safe horizon). Given a degradation rate $d$ and an acceptability threshold $c_{\min}$, the safe horizon $N^*(c, d)$ is the latest step at which the tacit state still has acceptable credence:
$$N^*(c, d) = \max\{n \in \mathbb{N} \mid c'(n) \geq c_{\min}\} = \left\lfloor \frac{c - c_{\min}}{d} \right\rfloor$$

**Theorem 5.8** (Safe horizon characterization). The safe horizon satisfies:
$$c'(N^*) \geq c_{\min} \wedge c'(N^*+1) < c_{\min}$$

**[Lean: `Frfp.Core.DynamicLayer.safe_horizon_characterization`]**

The safe horizon determines when the pipeline *must* invoke a $\mathrm{TE}$ or $\mathrm{HFD}$ step — i.e., when human evaluation is no longer optional but required to maintain tacit validity.

---

## 5.8 Tacit Irreversibility

**Theorem 5.9** (Tacit irreversibility). Once a pipeline transitions from explicit to tacit space (via $\mathrm{RB}$), this transition cannot be reversed:
$$\nexists\ \text{sequence of primitives from } T_0 \text{ to } E_0$$

This is a restatement of Theorem 1.7 (No-Return Principle) in dynamic terms: the degradation of tacit state is irreversible; once human evaluation is required, machine processing alone cannot restore tacit validity.
